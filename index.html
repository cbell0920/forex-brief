Macro Environment

The macro picture entering June 26 is clearer than it was 24 hours ago. PCE inflation confirmed at 4.1% YoY — the highest since April 2023 — with core PCE at 3.4%. The monthly headline came in at +0.4%, slightly below the +0.5% forecast, which provided modest relief but didn't change the fundamental narrative. The Fed's preferred inflation gauge has now crossed 4% for the first time in three years, driven by energy shock from the Iran war passing through the supply chain. Markets reacted with relative calm — equities closed higher Thursday as the in-line print wasn't the shock some feared, and Micron's AI earnings provided an offsetting positive catalyst.

The key takeaway for FX: the PCE print does not change the USD bull case, it confirms it. Warsh's Fed explicitly stated it will "deliver price stability" — the dot plot already showed 9 of 19 members expect at least one hike. September is 68% priced. The dollar's bid is structural, not just reactive. DXY at 101.3 has swept past chart resistance. The path toward 103–104 opens if inflation remains sticky.

There is one nuance worth noting: analysts expect May to mark the inflation peak. Crude oil has fallen from ~$114/bbl at the Iran war peak back to $69/bbl as the Strait of Hormuz has reopened. That disinflationary impulse hasn't shown up in PCE yet but should start to in June and July data. If PCE peaks at 4.1% and starts declining, the Fed hike narrative softens and the dollar rally may stall. This is not a reason to go long EUR/USD today — but it is a reason not to pile in with maximum conviction at current levels.

In the UK, political transition is accelerating. Reports indicate Andy Burnham is the likely successor to Keir Starmer as Labour leader. Questions about Rachel Reeves' future as Chancellor are circulating. This political uncertainty is a direct GBP headwind — not a fundamental rate story but a discount the market applies to GBP regardless.

Senior Analyst Note
TPTraders Senior FX Analyst — June 26, 2026

PCE came in at 4.1% — in line annually, slightly soft monthly at +0.4% vs +0.5% expected. Markets took the mild undershoot as a relief valve and equities bounced. For FX, nothing fundamentally changed. Core PCE at 3.4% YoY — highest since October 2023 — confirms the Fed cannot cut and is more likely to hike. The monthly softness is a single data point against a trend of rising inflation. The USD bull case is intact.

Today is Friday — that changes the trading calculus. End-of-week positioning flows can create false moves in both directions. Long USD positions that were built up through the week may be trimmed for the weekend, creating a temporary USD softness and bounces in EUR/USD and GBP/USD. These bounces are not reversals — they are entry opportunities. The setups to watch: EUR/USD into 1.1380–1.1420 is a short entry, not a breakout. GBP/USD into 1.3200–1.3220 is a short entry, not a reversal.

The cleanest weekend trade remains long USD/CAD. There are no CAD-positive catalysts on the horizon. Oil at $69 is a direct headwind. The political noise is all USD-positive. Specs are dangerously long CAD. If USD/CAD closes above 1.4180 today, the weekend positioning favors further USD/CAD upside into next week.

One thing to flag for the weeks ahead: if June PCE (reported in late July) shows inflation peaking or declining, the USD rally will lose its primary catalyst. The disinflationary effect of oil falling from $114 to $69 will start showing in June and July PCE data. The window for the highest-conviction USD longs may be the next 4–6 weeks before that data changes the narrative. Trade the dollar hard now, and reassess in mid-July when June PCE is approaching.
