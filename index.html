Macro Environment

The dominant post-super-week macro theme is a hawkish repricing of the Federal Reserve under new Chair Kevin Warsh. The June 17 FOMC held rates at 3.50–3.75% in a unanimous 12-0 vote, but the dot plot was a hawkish shock — the median year-end projection rose to 3.8% (from 3.4% in March). With US CPI at 4.2%, markets are now pricing a possible hike by October. The Dollar Index broke above 100 and has held there. This is the key structural driver across all USD pairs: the cutting cycle the market priced through early 2026 is now off the table, and the next move is more likely up than down.

The ECB raised its deposit facility rate to 2.25% on June 11 — its first hike since 2023 — in response to eurozone CPI at 3.2% in May with core at 2.5%. This limits EUR downside against GBP and JPY, but the ECB is fighting against a growth slowdown: the June composite PMI came in at 49.5, below 50 for a third consecutive month. The hawkish policy backdrop cannot fully offset the recessionary PMI signal, which is why EUR/USD remains under pressure from the stronger USD despite the ECB hike.

In the UK, the Bank of England held at 3.75% in a 7-2 vote on June 18. Two MPC members voted for a hike — UK services CPI at 3.6% is the culprit. Political uncertainty continues to weigh on GBP independently of the monetary policy picture. The BoE-ECB rate gap has narrowed to 150bp after the ECB hike, trimming GBP/EUR's structural support.

The Iran deal progress from last week has provided a disinflationary tailwind via lower oil prices, but markets are cautiously treating it as fragile. Gold has pulled back to ~$4,050 — a two-week low — as the USD strengthens and safe haven demand softens at the margins. The commodity complex remains a secondary rather than primary driver this week.

Today's PCE print is the single most important catalyst of the week. Wells Fargo is forecasting +0.5% headline m/m and +0.3% core m/m. A hot print confirms the October hike narrative and extends USD strength across all major pairs. A soft miss triggers a sharp reversal — expect 50–80 pip moves on EUR/USD and GBP/USD on the number.

Senior Analyst Note
TPTraders Senior FX Analyst — June 24, 2026

The dominant trade setup this week is short EUR/USD on any bounce toward 1.1450–1.1500. The ECB's hike to 2.25% should theoretically support the euro — and it does relative to GBP and JPY — but against the USD it's the weaker force. Three consecutive months of composite PMI below 50 tells you the eurozone economy is struggling to absorb the tightening. The Fed's hawkish dot plot under Warsh is the dominant narrative. The 1.1500 level is both structural resistance and the line in the sand for USD bears; if it caps, the path toward 1.1300 and ultimately 1.1150 opens up over the next 2–4 weeks.

USD/JPY is high conviction but high risk. The fundamental case — 275bp rate differential, BoJ still normalising slowly at 0.75% — argues for 163+. But the MoF is not bluffing at these levels. The last intervention came swiftly and without warning. Preferred approach: stay long USD/JPY but size conservatively — no more than 50% of normal size — and keep stops tight below 160.50. A breach of 163.00 on heavy volume opens 165. An MoF intervention drops the pair 200–300 pips in minutes.

USD/CAD is the cleanest long. Spec positioning is extremely long CAD, yet the macro picture is unambiguously bearish CAD — deteriorating real growth, the BoC wanting to cut but unable to, and USD dominance across the board. When spec longs get caught offside in a USD-bullish move, the unwind is fast and violent. USD/CAD above 1.4200 on a daily close confirms the break. Target 1.4400–1.4500 on a hot PCE print today.

PCE at 08:30 ET today determines the next leg. Hot (+0.5% headline / +0.3% core): add USD longs across the board, fade EUR and AUD bounces, stay with USD/CAD and USD/JPY positions. Soft miss: take profits, wait for re-entry — don't fight the reversal, the move can be sharp. The week's positioning bias is clear; today's data either accelerates it or gives you a better entry.
