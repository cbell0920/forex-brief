Hormuz Ceasefire Fractures and Warsh Goes Silent: Two Macro Shocks, One Session

Wednesday July 8 is the most event-dense session of the month so far. The operative macro shock is the collapse of the US-Iran Hormuz ceasefire: Iran attacked three commercial vessels on Tuesday, the US Treasury revoked Tehran's oil-sale waiver, and US CENTCOM confirmed retaliatory strikes overnight. WTI has surged +5.05% to $74.00 in response. This is a structural oil shock, not a one-day headline — the fragile MOU is now openly contested, and any further Iranian aggression could push WTI back toward the $80-90 range last seen in the acute phase of the conflict. For FX, the primary transmission mechanism is unambiguously CAD-bullish: USD/CAD has broken from 1.4250 toward 1.4171 and any sustained WTI above $75 sets a structural path toward 1.4100. CAD/JPY at 114.65 has room for 115.50 if the oil bid persists. Concurrently, precious metals are collapsing — gold at $4,062.70 (-2.28%) and silver at $58.90 (-3.96%) — a counterintuitive move that signals rising real-yield dominance over geopolitical safe-haven demand.

The second shock is purely monetary: the FOMC minutes for the June 16-17 meeting release at 14:00 ET today. These are not routine minutes. Chair Warsh withheld his own dot-plot submission — the first Fed chair to do so — meaning the transcript is the FOMC's sole on-record statement about whether a September rate hike is coming. The dot-plot is already deeply divided: 9 of 18 officials see at least one 2026 hike, 8 see no change, 1 sees a cut. Core PCE was revised to 3.3% for 2026, and Warsh has publicly stated the Fed will not accept above-2% inflation. If the minutes reveal hawkish committee language supporting a September move, DXY — currently at 101.14 — has a clear technical path to retest the 101.70-102.00 zone per pre-NFP highs. This would press EUR/USD back toward the 1.1350 June low and extend USD/JPY above 163.00 against a structurally weak yen (BoJ at 1.00%, leveraged funds net short ~115,000 contracts at June 30). Conversely, dovish minutes that emphasise the June payrolls miss (only 57K) and internal hesitation on hiking would compress hike odds below 40%, sending EUR/USD back above 1.1450 and providing relief to gold.

The RBNZ hike to 2.50% delivered today adds a third layer: NZD is the session's best-performing major currency at +0.35% (NZD/USD 0.5698), with NZD/JPY the standout at +0.58% (92.53). However, Commerzbank and FXStreet analysis both flag that the market's ~3.5-hike pricing over 12 months is excessive — Breman's press conference struck a cautious 'feeling our way to neutral' tone. Fade-the-hike trades on AUD/NZD (currently 1.2146) and EUR/NZD (2.0027 testing the 2.0000 handle) offer interesting setups on a 1-2 week horizon, particularly if FOMC minutes are hawkish enough to reassert USD as the dominant rate-hike narrative and reduce relative NZD appeal.

The clearest setups for today's session: (1) Buy USD/CAD below 1.4200 with a stop above 1.4280, targeting 1.4100, provided WTI holds above $72 — the energy shock and pre-minutes USD consolidation align. (2) Fade NZD/USD at 0.5700 resistance for a re-test of 0.5650 if FOMC minutes lean hawkish — the round number rejection and excessive hike pricing provide the setup. (3) Stay long CAD/JPY above 113.50 targeting 115.50 — this is the cleanest expression of the dual oil-surge/yen-weakness macro regime. Risk events: any US-Iran de-escalation headline (would rapidly flip CAD negative), FOMC minutes decidedly dovish (reverses USD strength across the board), and tonight's BoJ commentary on potential intervention at 162+ USD/JPY.
