Senior Analyst · TPTraders FX Desk · Friday, July 10, 2026
Yen at 161.85, MOF on standby, and a CPI print that could redraw the entire macro map — Friday is not for the faint-hearted.

The dominant theme entering this Friday session is the collision between a structurally rangebound DXY (100.86, -0.04%) and a set of currency crosses that are anything but stable. The JPY complex deserves the most attention: USD/JPY at 161.85 is sitting less than 20 pips below the 162.00 level that analysts at T. Rowe Price identified as the MOF's likely re-entry trigger, and large speculator yen shorts are at their most extreme since July 2024 per the latest COT data. Japan's MoF deployed a record ¥11.73 trillion ($73.5 billion) in April-May after USD/JPY breached 160, yet the pair returned above that level within six weeks — a worrying precedent suggesting intervention is a speed bump, not a wall. However, with the BoJ now at 1.00% following June's 25bp hike and gross JPY longs building, the conditions for a more durable yen recovery may finally be assembling. Traders should keep position sizes tight in USD/JPY and EUR/JPY (185.00) today with asymmetric MOF risk to the downside.

On the USD side, the June CPI print at 08:30 ET is the session's pivotal data point. The May CPI came in at 4.17% YoY, and year-ahead inflation expectations remain elevated at 4.6% per the latest Michigan survey (June final: 49.5). Fed Chair Warsh at Sintra on July 1 said 'prices are too high' while declining to hint at a July move at the upcoming July 29 FOMC meeting — cementing the data-dependent stance. A hot CPI above 4.2% would reinforce the case for a December hike and provide the catalyst DXY needs to break above 101.50; a soft print below 3.8% would ignite a commodity FX rally with AUD/USD targeting 0.7000 and NZD/USD potentially triggering a violent short-squeeze from record-short COT positioning.

The GBP setup is perhaps the cleanest structural trade on the board. GBP/USD at 1.3426 is the session's top major performer, and the COT backdrop is unusually constructive: asset manager net-shorts are at a record high of -156.3k contracts, yet gross longs are simultaneously at a record — a classic bimodal sentiment extreme that often precedes a squeeze. The BoE next meets July 30, with a hold expected, but BNP Paribas flags a September hike as the base case. A sustained hold above 1.3400 pivots the near-term target to 1.3480 and then the 1.3500 psychological level. GBP/CHF (1.0830, +0.20%) is the cleanest expression of this theme given CHF net-shorts are simultaneously at a 7-week high.

Cross-asset risk is clearly skewing cautious: the Fear & Greed index at 23/100 (Extreme Fear), silver -1.32%, and gold -0.60% despite geopolitical uncertainty all suggest that the equity-linked risk-on bid is absent today. The Michigan Sentiment preliminary at 10:00 ET will be the final significant release, with the market expecting a recovery to ~52 from June's 49.5. Consumer inflation expectations holding at 4.6% remain the critical sub-component — any further de-anchoring higher would be a material risk event for Treasuries and by extension the USD. The clearest trades for today: fade USD/JPY above 162.00 with tight stops at 162.50, buy GBP dips toward 1.3380 ahead of the squeeze setup, and watch USD/CAD at 1.4157 for a clean break to 1.4100 if Canada employment beats and CPI surprises to the downside.
