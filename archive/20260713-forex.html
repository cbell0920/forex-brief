Hormuz Fires the Starting Gun: Oil at $74.12, EUR at Squeeze-Ready 1.1432, and Three Central Banks This Week

The week opens with a macro cocktail that is simultaneously bullish oil, bearish risk, and ambiguous for the US dollar. WTI crude surged +3.79% to $74.12 as US-Iran hostilities re-escalated over the weekend — the fourth American strike in a week, Iran's contested Strait of Hormuz closure claim, and three tanker attacks since July 8 collectively represent the most significant geopolitical oil shock in months. With the Strait carrying ~20% of global oil trade and the EIA's July STEO (published July 7) having factored in a normalising Hormuz from the June 18 MOU, markets are now violently repricing the supply risk premium back in. The direct FX implication is CAD-bullish (USD/CAD -0.12% at 1.4135, with $74 WTI likely to keep the pair capped below 1.4200) and JPY/CHF-supportive as safe-haven demand competes with broader USD resilience anchored by a hawkish Fed at 3.50-3.75%.

The clearest high-conviction setup this week centres on Tuesday's US CPI (June), where the consensus forecast of 3.8% YoY versus 4.2% prior represents the largest single-month deceleration in headline inflation in 2026. If realised, this would materially reduce the probability of an FOMC hike at the July 28-29 meeting — a DXY-negative outcome that could propel EUR/USD through the 1.1500 resistance and GBP/USD toward 1.3450. The EUR setup is particularly compelling from a positioning standpoint: COT non-commercial nets have flipped marginally net-short (-16K) for the first time in this dataset cycle, placing EUR at the -2.00σ Extreme Short threshold — a level that has historically preceded sharp short-covering rallies. EUR/USD at 1.1432 is sitting on the 1.1400 pivot; a soft CPI tomorrow provides the fundamental catalyst the COT picture is primed for. Conversely, a CPI beat above 4.0% combined with hawkish Warsh testimony before the House Financial Services Committee — his first Congressional appearance since being sworn in May 22 — would reaffirm the hawkish Fed narrative, push DXY back above 101.50, and send EUR/USD back toward 1.1350 and GBP/USD toward 1.3300.

The secondary theme is the synchronized central bank convergence in the final two weeks of July: BoC (July 15) expected to hold at 2.25% but risk a dovish MPR tone given USMCA renegotiation uncertainty; ECB (July 23) on hold following June's hike with Lagarde's neutral bias firmly established; FOMC (July 29) on hold with a residual 32% hike pricing; BoE (July 30) anchored at 3.75% with Bailey firmly dovish; and BoJ (July 31) — the potential wildcard. The BoJ raised to 1.00% in June, its third hike since January 2025, and the COT data now shows the single largest weekly JPY short cover (+31,314 contracts) in the dataset, breaking from the -1.91σ extreme. This is the clearest positioning reversal signal of the month — if the BoJ delivers even mildly hawkish language on July 31, the JPY short unwind that has been building over five weeks could be violent, compressing EUR/JPY from 185.32 back toward 182.00 and potentially pushing USD/JPY (implied ~162.10) sharply lower toward 158-160.

Finally, Wednesday's China Q2 GDP (forecast 4.4% YoY vs 5.0% prior) is the commodity-FX swing factor. AUD/USD at 0.6944 is already under pressure with COT net-short at a 24-week high; a GDP miss below 4.4% would expose the 0.6900 support and accelerate EUR/AUD toward 1.66. Copper at $6.3010 is the real-time China sentiment gauge to watch — silver's -2.40% decline to $58.72 is already flashing a broader risk-off warning. The week's risk hierarchy is clear: CPI Tuesday > Warsh testimony Tuesday > China GDP Wednesday > BoC Wednesday > BoJ positioning into July 31. With Fear & Greed at 28 and gold at $4,068.70 already pulling back from extremes, this is not the week to be complacent with large directional exposure into any of these events.
