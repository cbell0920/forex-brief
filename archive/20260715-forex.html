Senior Analyst · TPTraders FX Desk · Wednesday, July 15, 2026
CPI Defanged But Warsh Has the Fangs — Today's Senate Testimony Is the Real Market Mover

Yesterday's June CPI bombshell — headline -0.4% MoM and 3.5% YoY against expectations of -0.2%/3.8% — should have been a watershed dovish catalyst. Instead, DXY is clinging to 100.98, EUR/USD is barely holding 1.1418, and gold is retreating to $4,045. The reason is simple: Chair Warsh's pre-testimony remarks explicitly rejected any 'mission accomplished' framing, and nine FOMC members' June dots already penciled in a year-end rate hike. The June CPI was largely an energy story — the energy index fell 5.7%, its biggest monthly drop since April 2020, driven by the brief US-Iran ceasefire in June. But that ceasefire is now unraveling. Trump's reimposition of a Strait of Hormuz blockade has already sent Brent futures up ~10% in a single session, and WTI at $79.54 today is just the beginning if Hormuz disruption materialises fully. If July CPI re-accelerates — which is structurally likely given WTI's trajectory — Warsh's 'data dependent with no tolerance for persistent inflation' framework effectively becomes a September hike justification. The clearest near-term setup is therefore to sell EUR/USD rallies into 1.1450, where COT positioning (first net-short in 3 years, Z1=-2.00σ Extreme Short) and the Warsh hawkish pivot converge as headwinds. A break below 1.1380 would open a run toward 1.1300. On the JPY crosses, USD/JPY at 162.33 remains the most dangerous pair on the board: COT shows the single largest weekly JPY short-cover in the dataset history (+31,314 WoW) coinciding with BoJ Deputy Governor Himino explicitly flagging upside inflation risk and a tightening bias toward the 2% neutral rate. MOF has previously intervened near these levels (last intervention May 6), and USD/JPY trading above 163.00 would likely trigger coordinated action. The reward-to-risk on short USD/JPY from 162.33 toward 160.50 is compelling if Warsh delivers a dovish surprise today or CPI-driven risk-on takes hold. The highest-conviction cross-market trade today is the BoC MPR at 09:45 ET, where Governor Macklem faces an impossible task: inflation above 3% from Iran war oil, a labour market deteriorating toward 7% unemployment, and CUSMA trade review creating investment paralysis. CAD COT is at a record short of -173,126 contracts — the most bearish CAD positioning in the 3-year dataset. If Macklem's tone is even marginally less dovish than feared (referencing inflation risks over growth risks), USD/CAD could snap lower from 1.4068 toward 1.4020, and GBP/CAD's outperformance (+0.25% today) could stall sharply. Conversely, any growth-focused language that opens the door to future rate cuts = CAD/JPY breaks below 115.00 and USD/CAD pushes toward 1.4130. Finally, the silver underperformance (-1.06%) relative to gold (-0.60%) with copper flat at $6.374 sends a clear cross-asset message: do not read the AUD/USD move toward 0.7000 as genuine risk-on. It is a COT positioning unwind (AUD longs near record vs. NZD/CAD record shorts) rather than a fundamental growth signal. The Fear & Greed index at 25/100 (Extreme Fear) and Middle East geopolitical escalation risk mean any AUD/USD long above 0.6990 requires strict risk management with stops below 0.6940.
