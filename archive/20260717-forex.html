Copper Cracks the Aussie While JPY Carry Holds Near 40-Year Lows — Structure Tells Two Clear Stories

The dominant theme into this Friday's close is a clean divergence between commodity-linked and carry-funded pairs. On the commodity side, copper's 1.89% collapse is the sharpest single intraday mover in today's data set and it is landing directly on AUD/USD at 0.6972 — the pair's -0.37% session loss is the largest among the majors and tracks copper's weakness almost tick for tick. AUD/JPY at 113.14, AUD/CAD at 0.9783, and AUD/CHF at 0.5629 are all off between 0.34% and 0.37%, forming a uniform band of Aussie weakness that is commodity-driven rather than broad risk-off. Critically, the IOF weekly structure on AUD/USD, AUD/JPY, AUD/CAD, and AUD/CHF is all Bullish — meaning the two most recently completed weekly candles still reflect higher-highs and higher-lows. Today's intraday pressure is therefore working against the prevailing weekly trend, not confirming a break of it. The same structural backdrop applies to NZD/USD, NZD/JPY, NZD/CAD, and NZD/CHF — all Bullish — though NZD is outperforming AUD sharply today, with NZD/USD off only 0.07% versus AUD's 0.37%. The AUD/NZD cross at 1.1949 (-0.20%) captures this intra-commodity-bloc rotation precisely.

On the carry and Sterling side, USD/JPY at 162.33 is virtually unchanged and represents the session's most striking stasis given the geopolitical backdrop and Fear & Greed at 27. The BoJ holds at 0.75% — the highest since 1995 — but that rate is still nowhere near sufficient to unwind the Fed differential, with US CPI at 3.5% and Chair Warsh explicitly framing price stability as the Fed's primary mandate. The April BoJ meeting's 6-3 vote split calling for an immediate hike to 1% is the real catalyst to monitor heading into the July 30-31 BoJ meeting, just days after the Fed's own July 29 decision. GBP/USD at 1.3434 (-0.34%) is the weakest major on the session and GBP/JPY at 218.07 (-0.31%) tells the same story — sterling is underperforming across the board. GBP/USD, GBP/JPY, GBP/CAD, and GBP/CHF all carry Bullish weekly structure, so today's Sterling softness is again occurring within, not against, the broader weekly trend. EUR/GBP at 0.8507 (+0.25%) is the clearest expression of intraday sterling underperformance, and its Bearish weekly structure — lower-highs and lower-lows on the two most recent completed weekly candles — aligns with the cross's longer-term trajectory favouring GBP over EUR even as today's session runs the other direction.

On the Bearish-structure side, EUR/AUD at 1.6395 and EUR/NZD at 1.9593 both carry Bearish weekly structure, meaning EUR has been making lower-highs and lower-lows relative to both antipodean currencies on a multi-week basis — a notable contrast to today's session where EUR is actually the stronger party. USD/CAD at 1.4037 also holds Bearish weekly structure, consistent with WTI oil's 2.04% gain today at $79.88 providing ongoing CAD support; the oil-CAD correlation is one of the more reliable structural links in FX and it is actively in play this session. CHF/JPY at 200.98 carries Bearish weekly structure — the one notable exception among the JPY crosses, suggesting recent weekly price action has not favoured CHF on a relative basis versus the yen despite today's modest +0.11% CHF/JPY gain.

Looking ahead to the 10:00 ET UoM Consumer Sentiment preliminary, June's final reading came in at 49.5 with year-ahead inflation expectations still at 4.6% — historically elevated and a key input into the Fed's reaction function under Warsh. A re-acceleration in inflation expectations would be a near-term USD catalyst, particularly for EUR/USD which sits at 1.1431 with Mixed weekly structure offering no structural edge in either direction. Mixed structure also applies to USD/JPY and EUR/JPY — pairs where the weekly candle comparison has not produced the clean higher-high/higher-low or lower-high/lower-low sequences needed to confirm trend. With two consequential central bank meetings — Fed on July 29 and BoJ on July 30-31 — both sitting just twelve trading days away, today's data prints and Vice Chair Jefferson's speech carry added weight as markets begin pricing the forward path.
