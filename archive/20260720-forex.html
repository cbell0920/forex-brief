Commodity Currencies Surge as EUR Struggles Into a Week Packed With Tier-1 Risk

Monday opens with a clear divergence in weekly structure across the major pairs, and the price action so far is consistent with those directional reads. AUD/USD (0.7012, +0.39%), NZD/USD (0.5858, +0.22%), GBP/USD (1.3462, +0.07%), NZD/CAD, NZD/JPY, NZD/CHF, AUD/JPY, AUD/CHF, GBP/JPY, GBP/CHF, CAD/JPY, CAD/CHF, EUR/JPY, and EUR/CHF all carry bullish weekly structure — a cluster that broadly reflects a risk-on commodity currency bid and relative GBP and JPY resilience, even as the overarching sentiment gauge sits at 29/100 (Fear). The AUD is today's standout, and the copper +1.01% and gold +0.30% session gains dovetail neatly with that read: commodity-linked demand is lending the Aussie a dual tailwind into a week that includes Thursday's Australian employment data.

On the bearish structure side, EUR/USD, EUR/AUD, EUR/NZD, EUR/CAD, EUR/GBP, GBP/AUD, GBP/NZD, GBP/CAD, AUD/NZD, AUD/CAD, and CHF/JPY all show lower-high, lower-low weekly sequences — and the EUR bears that story most forcefully this session with EUR/USD slipping to 1.1432 (-0.12%). The asymmetric risk for EUR this week is Thursday's ECB decision: with a hold at the current 2.25% deposit facility rate almost fully priced, the volatility event is Christine Lagarde's press conference tone on September tightening. A hawkish signal reinforcing further hikes would be an unusual catalyst for EUR strength; a neutral or ambiguous tone risks accelerating the EUR's existing bearish structure trajectory. USD/CAD at 1.4036 also carries bearish weekly structure, and today's Canada CPI print (June, consensus 2.8% YoY vs. 3.2% prior) is the immediate event risk — a figure in line with or below forecast would align with the structure read for USD/CAD, while a surprise to the upside near or above 3.0% would cut against it.

USD/JPY is the notable gap in this brief — live data is unavailable — but the JPY crosses offer a reasonable proxy. EUR/JPY at 185.54 carries bullish weekly structure, while CHF/JPY at 200.93 shows bearish structure, pointing to a nuanced JPY environment rather than a clean directional narrative. The BoJ hiked to 1.00% in June in a 7-1 vote and Deputy Governor Himino has retained a tightening bias, with the next meeting due July 30-31. The carry unwind risk is real but markets are pricing only marginal further tightening at the upcoming meeting, suggesting JPY remains a slow-burn story rather than a session catalyst today.

For context: the IOF weekly structure across all pairs this session aligns notably well with the commodity currency-led risk tone visible in the spot prices. With Canada CPI, PBOC LPR, NZ Q2 CPI (Tuesday), UK jobs (Tuesday), UK CPI (Wednesday), ECB (Thursday), Australian jobs (Thursday), and global PMIs (Friday) all landing within the next five trading days, today's relatively quiet Monday session may be the calmest window of the week — the structure reads give a framework, but the calendar ahead will determine whether the current directional flows extend or reverse.
